2026 NFL offensive line rankings: Cowboys surge, Chargers dip after Week 3
By Gordon McGuinness
Posted Sep 30, 2026 6:00 am EDT
  • The Cowboys jump 10 spots: Growth from Tyler Guyton as well as Tyler Booker has set Dallas up for success, even without Tyler Smith.
  • The Chargers' freefall continues: Woes along the interior offensive line see Los Angeles plummet out of the top 10.

The offensive line is arguably the most important unit in football. The group, or even a single weak link within it, can derail a season quickly.

Throughout the season, I will be ranking every offensive line in the NFL each week. That includes updating the list as we learn more about each player as the year wears on, and groups change due to injuries or players returning.

Here are the updated PFF offensive line rankings after Week 2 of the NFL season. For all grades and metrics, including pass-blocking win rate, positively and negatively graded run-block rates, island rate and island win rate, subscribe to PFF Pro.

Methodology

To score each offensive line, I first gave each player a grade between 5 and 10 in 0.5 increments, using their PFF grades over the past three years where applicable to shape that score, with a weighting toward pass protection. I then got an average for the group, with a slight weighting toward the left and right tackles to reach a mark out of 10 for each team in the league.

Over the first four weeks of the season, I will only update the rankings based on lineup changes. That’s because the sample size in the first four weeks of the year is quite small. As we add each week and learn more about 2026 performance, we'll use that to increase or decrease each player's score.

1. Denver Broncos (no change)

  • LT: Garett Bolles
  • LG: Ben Powers
  • C: Luke Wattenberg
  • RG: Quinn Meinerz
  • RT: Mike McGlinchey

Garett Bolles was Denver’s best offensive lineman in the win over the Los Angeles Rams on Sunday Night Football, allowing just one pressure from 41 pass-blocking snaps. The unit impressed as run-blockers too, with four of their five starters securing PFF run-blocking grades above 65.0, and three of them at 70.0 or better.

2. Tampa Bay Buccaneers (no change)

  • LT: Tristan Wirfs
  • LG: Ben Bredeson
  • C: Graham Barton
  • RG: Cody Mauch
  • RT: Luke Goedeke

The Buccaneers had a good game in pass protection against the Minnesota Vikings, with both starting tackles and left guard Ben Bredeson allowing one or fewer pressures from 50 pass-blocking snaps. However, Tampa Bay did struggle in the running game, with none of the group earning a PFF grade above 55.0 in that department.

3. Indianapolis Colts (no change)

  • LT: Bernhard Raimann
  • LG: Quenton Nelson
  • C: Tanor Bortolini
  • RG: Matt Goncalves
  • RT: Jalen Travis

Left tackle Bernhard Raimann had an excellent day in pass protection, producing an 88.2 PFF pass-blocking grade and allowing just one pressure from 41 pass-blocking snaps. Given the level of competition he was up against in the Houston pass rush, that’s an incredible stat line to produce. Yet right tackle Jalen Travis had much less success, yielding 11 total pressures including a combined four sacks and quarterback hits.

4. Philadelphia Eagles (no change)

  • LT: Jordan Mailata
  • LG: Cam Jurgens
  • C: Drew Kendall
  • RG: Tyler Steen
  • RT: Lane Johnson

The Eagles’ starting offensive line allowed seven pressures in the loss to the Chicago Bears on Monday Night Football, with four of them coming from left guard Cam Jurgens. The other four starters combined to permit just three. Jurgens has struggled much more in pass protection in two games at left guard than he did in Week 1 at center, with the knock on impact of the Landon Dickerson injury giving this offensive line some problems.

5. Chicago Bears (no change)

  • LT: Braxton Jones
  • LG: Joe Thuney
  • C: Garrett Bradbury
  • RG: Jonah Jackson
  • RT: Darnell Wright

The Bears’ offensive line had another strong performance in pass protection in the win over the Eagles on Monday Night Football. Darnell Wright continues to look like an absolute superstar at right tackle, though the concern coming out of this game was left tackle Braxton Jones leaving with a knee injury. Theo Benedet underwhelmed in pass protection in relief of him against the Eagles and would likely start if Jones misses time.

6. Buffalo Bills (no change)

  • LT: Dion Dawkins
  • LG: Alec Anderson
  • C: Connor McGovern
  • RG: O’Cyrus Torrence
  • RT: Spencer Brown

The Bills got a mixed performance from their offensive line in the comeback win over the Los Angeles Chargers on Sunday afternoon. Spencer Brown was excellent at right tackle, impressing as a run-blocker and allowing zero pressures from 33 pass-blocking snaps. Left tackle Dion Dawkins did give up three pressures but was dominant as a run-blocker, receiving a positive grade on 25.0% of his snaps compared with a negative grade on just 6.3%.

7. Atlanta Falcons (no change)

  • LT: Jake Matthews
  • LG: Matthew Bergeron
  • C: Ryan Neuzil
  • RG: Chris Lindstrom
  • RT: Jawaan Taylor

Both of the Falcons' guards shined in the Thursday Night Football win over the Green Bay Packers, with Chris Lindstrom allowing zero pressures from 30 pass-blocking snaps, and Matthew Bergeron responsible for two on the same snap count. Lindstrom was once again Atlanta’s best pass protector, producing a 0.0% negative graded play rate in the win.

8. San Francisco 49ers (no change)

  • LT: Trent Williams
  • LG: Connor Colby
  • C: Jake Brendel
  • RG: Dominick Puni
  • RT: Colton McKivitz

The 49ers’ offensive line was key to their win over the Arizona Cardinals in Week 3, but the best thing for them on Sunday was the news that Trent Williams’ injury is not expected to be long-term. Verderian Lowe replaced him in the lineup when Williams went down and struggled significantly, earning PFF pass- and run-blocking grades below 50.0.

9. Los Angeles Rams (up 1)

  • LT: Alaric Jackson
  • LG: Steve Avila
  • C: Coleman Shelton
  • RG: Kevin Dotson
  • RT: Warren McClendon Jr.

The Rams’ offensive line had issues against a fantastic Denver Broncos defense on Sunday Night Football, with their highest-graded offensive lineman in the game right tackle Warren McClendon Jr. The group combined to allow 21 pressures against Denver, with center Coleman Shelton the only starter who didn’t give up multiple.

10. Minnesota Vikings (up 1)

  • LT: Christian Darrisaw
  • LG: Donovan Jackson
  • C: Blake Brandel
  • RG: Will Fries
  • RT: Brian O’Neill

The Vikings’ offensive line did a stellar job in the Week 3 win over the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, particularly in pass protection. All five starters earned PFF pass-blocking grades above 68.0, and left guard Donovan Jackson was the only player to allow multiple pressures. Minnesota had three players generate sub-50.0 PFF grades as run-blockers, but both starting guards were above 80.0 in that facet.

11. Pittsburgh Steelers (up 1)

  • LT: Troy Fautanu
  • LG: Mason McCormick
  • C: Zach Frazier
  • RG: Brock Hoffman
  • RT: Max Iheanachor

The Steelers made multiple changes to their offensive line ahead of Week 3, and it will see them vault up these rankings with a repeat performance on Thursday Night Football against the Browns. Rookie right tackle Max Iheanachor started the first game of his career at right tackle, earning a 91.4 PFF run-blocking grade which led the team and allowing just two pressures. Next to him at right guard, Brock Hoffman got his first start and didn’t yield a pressure from 40 pass-blocking snaps.

12. Seattle Seahawks (up 1)

  • LT: Charles Cross
  • LG: Grey Zabel
  • C: Jalen Sundell
  • RG: Christian Haynes/Beau Stephens
  • RT: Abraham Lucas

The Seahawks once again platooned at right guard, with Christian Haynes and Beau Stephens splitting snaps in relief of Anthony Bradford, who is on injured reserve. Neither allowed pressure in the game from a joint 50 pass-blocking snaps. The Commanders found success against left guard Grey Zabel and right tackle Abraham Lucas, though, with the duo combining to allow nine of the 11 pressures surrendered by the Seattle offensive line.

13. Kansas City Chiefs (up 1)

  • LT: Kahlil Benson
  • LG: Kingsley Suamataia
  • C: Creed Humphrey
  • RG: Trey Smith
  • RT: Jaylon Moore

Despite the win against the Miami Dolphins, there were some worrisome signs for the Chiefs’ offensive line this week. Left tackle Kahlil Benson, still starting in relief of Josh Simmons, was one of three starters to earn sub-60.0 PFF grades both in pass protection and as run-blockers. But the right side of the line in Trey Smith and Jaylon Moore both had strong games in pass protection, with Moore allowing zero pressures and Smith one.

14. Dallas Cowboys (up 10)

  • LT: Tyler Guyton
  • LG: T.J. Bass
  • C: Cooper Beebe
  • RG: Tyler Booker
  • RT: Terence Steele

Tyler Guyton has taken a significant step forward so far this year, and the Cowboys have the chance to have a really potent left side of the line when left guard Tyler Smith returns from injury. It’s fair to wonder that even when Smith returns, Tyler Booker will still be the best player on this offensive line. The second-year guard earned an 86.1 PFF run-blocking grade in the loss to the Baltimore Ravens on Sunday.

15. Las Vegas Raiders (no change)

  • LT: Kolton Miller
  • LG: Spencer Burford
  • C: Tyler Linderbaum
  • RG: Jackson Powers-Johnson
  • RT: DJ Glaze

The Raiders’ offensive line has been a big part of why they are 3-0 to start the 2026 season, and the unit was excellent in pass protection once again in the win over the New Orleans Saints. As a group, Las Vegas allowed nine pressures in the game, but six of those were charted against left guard Spencer Burford and three against left tackle Kolton Miller. The three other starters did not permit a single pressure in the game.

16. Detroit Lions (no change)

  • LT: Penei Sewell
  • LG: Christian Mahogany
  • C: Juice Scruggs
  • RG: Tate Ratledge
  • RT: Blake Miller

Penei Sewell looked much more comfortable at left tackle in the Lions’ win over the New York Jets on Sunday, with no pressures allowed from 37 pass-blocking snaps. Opposite him, rookie Blake Miller was impressive again by giving up just two pressures, while both guards compiled PFF pass-blocking grades of 88.5. All five starters also finished with PFF run-blocking grades above 60.0.

17. Cincinnati Bengals (no change)

  • LT: Orlando Brown Jr.
  • LG: Dylan Fairchild
  • C: Ted Karras
  • RG: Dalton Risner
  • RT: Amarius Mims

Right tackle Amarius Mims continued his stellar start to the season in pass protection, earning a 75.8 PFF pass-blocking grade and allowing zero pressures. Through the first three weeks of the season, he has a 0.0% pressure rate given up and has produced a 95.5% PFF pass-block win rate. Cincinnati’s pass protection as a team was good in this game, with the unit combining to let up just seven pressures from 41 pass-blocking snaps.

18. Los Angeles Chargers (down 9)

  • LT: Rashawn Slater
  • LG: Trevor Penning
  • C: Jake Slaughter
  • RG: Cole Strange
  • RT: Joe Alt

Despite the loss on Sunday, the good news for the Chargers was that right tackle Joe Alt had his best game of the year, earning a 90.0 PFF run-blocking grade and allowing zero pressures from 43 pass-blocking snaps. The bad news is that the interior of this offensive line continues to be its Achilles heel, with all three starters this week netting PFF pass-blocking grades below 50.0.

19. New York Jets (down 1)

  • LT: Olu Fashanu
  • LG: Dylan Parham
  • C: Josh Myers
  • RG: Joe Tippman
  • RT: Armand Membou

For the first time this season, we saw the Jets’ talented offensive tackle duo struggle, with both Olu Fashanu and Armand Membou recording PFF pass-blocking grades below 60.0. The Detroit Lions had a lot of success against the left side of this line in particular, with Fashanu allowing six pressures and the combination of Dylan Parham and Jordan Meredith, who replaced the former after he left the game injured, letting up another six.

20. New Orleans Saints (down 1)

  • LT: Asim Richards
  • LG: David Edwards
  • C: Erik McCoy
  • RG: Cesar Ruiz
  • RT: Taliese Fuaga

The Saints’ offensive line did a solid overall job against the Las Vegas Raiders, with all but one of their offensive linemen receiving PFF grades of 65.0 or better. The lone weak link was left tackle Asim Richards, who was starting in place of the injured Kelvin Banks Jr. Richards permitted six of the nine pressures surrendered by the offensive line. It’s not unreasonable to suggest that a healthy Banks could have had the Saints in position to win this game given how everyone else played.

21. Arizona Cardinals (down 1)

  • LT: Paris Johnson Jr.
  • LG: Isaac Seumalo
  • C: Hjalte Froholdt
  • RG: Isaiah Adams
  • RT: Elijah Wilkinson

The left side of the Cardinals’ offensive line was just fine against the San Francisco 49ers, but the group struggled elsewhere. Center Hjalte Froholdt earned sub-55.0 PFF grades both in pass protection and as a run-blocker. Meanwhile, right-side starters Isaiah Adams and Elijah Wilkinson played to PFF run-blocking grades below 55.0, though they had better days in pass protection.

22. New York Giants (down 1)

  • LT: Andrew Thomas
  • LG: Jon Runyan
  • C: John Michael Schmitz Jr.
  • RG: Francis Mauigoa
  • RT: Jermaine Eluemunor

With the exception of starting center John Michael Schmitz Jr., who earned a PFF pass-blocking grade of just 19.0 this week, the Giants had a good day in pass protection against the Tennessee Titans. Both starting tackles compiled PFF pass-blocking grades above 80.0, with Andrew Thomas allowing one pressure from 27 pass-blocking snaps and Jermaine Eluemunor zero. Rookie right guard Francis Mauigoa sported the highest grade of his three-week career, yielding just one pressure in the game. 

23. New England Patriots (down 1)

  • LT: Will Campbell
  • LG: Alijah Vera-Tucker
  • C: Jared Wilson
  • RG: Greg Van Roten
  • RT: Morgan Moses

With a blowout loss to the Jacksonville Jaguars on Sunday, we got our first look at first-round tackle Caleb Lomu, who came in for veteran Morgan Moses for the final 20 snaps of the game. He allowed a pair of pressures from 14 pass-blocking snaps and earned a positive grade on 16.7% of his run-blocking snaps. From what Mike Vrabel said after the game, there is no suggestion that New England is looking to make a permanent move quite yet, but Lomu certainly didn’t look out of place on an NFL field.

24. Baltimore Ravens (down 1)

  • LT: Ronnie Stanley
  • LG: John Simpson
  • C: Olaivavega Ioane
  • RG: Andrew Vorhees
  • RT: Roger Rosengarten

With Jovaughn Gwyn and Ethan Pocic both injured — joining Danny Pinter — the Ravens have lost all three of their starting contenders at center from the start of preseason. Plan D will see them move rookie right guard Vega Ioane to center, where he finished the game on Sunday. Ioane performed well outside of some slightly inaccurate snaps, but transitioning him to center likely means starting Andrew Vorhees at right guard. There was a reason why Baltimore upgraded from him as a starter this offseason.

25. Miami Dolphins (no change)

  • LT: Patrick Paul
  • LG: Kadyn Proctor
  • C: Aaron Brewer
  • RG: Jonah Savaiinaea
  • RT: Austin Jackson

The Dolphins gave the Chiefs a scare for much of the game on Sunday, and a big part of that was the pass protection from the left side of the offensive line. Left tackle Patrick Paul and left guard Kadyn Proctor both garnered PFF pass-blocking grades above 70.0 and allowed just one pressure each from 46 pass-blocking snaps. The right side of the line was a different story, though, with both right guard Jonah Savaiinaea and right tackle Austin Jackson notching sub-60.0 PFF pass-blocking grades and each surrendering three pressures on those same 46 snaps.

26. Carolina Panthers (no change)

  • LT: Rasheed Walker
  • LG: Damien Lewis
  • C: Luke Fortner
  • RG: Robert Hunt
  • RT: Monroe Freeling

The Panthers’ backup tackles looked as such this week, with Rasheed Walker and Monroe Freeling both allowing six pressures in the loss to the Cleveland Browns. Right guard Robert Hunt also gave up six pressures, with left guard Damien Lewis the only starter to be responsible for fewer than three. Lewis missed time during the game through injury, so that is something to monitor as his absence would be a big blow.

27. Jacksonville Jaguars (no change)

  • LT: Anton Harrison
  • LG: Emmanuel Pregnon
  • C: Robert Hainsey
  • RG: Ezra Cleveland
  • RT: Cole Van Lanen

Left tackle Anton Harrison had his best game of the season for the Jaguars on Sunday, earning a 91.3 PFF run-blocking grade and also allowing just one pressure in pass protection. On the right side of the line, Cole Van Lanen didn’t have a great game, posting just a 46.7 PFF grade and giving up three pressures.

28. Tennessee Titans (no change)

  • LT: Dan Moore Jr.
  • LG: Peter Skoronski
  • C: Austin Schlottmann
  • RG: Fernando Carmona Jr.
  • RT: JC Latham

Right guard Fernando Carmona Jr. was initially questionable for this game but did indeed start. However, he suffered an injury on the very first play of the game and was replaced by Jackson Slater. The early word from the Titans this week is that they expect Carmona to be in the lineup in Week 4. The group didn’t allow much pressure against the Giants, with just three combined pressures charged to the group from 39 pass-blocking snaps.

29. Cleveland Browns (no change)

  • LT: Spencer Fano
  • LG: Zion Johnson
  • C: Elgton Jenkins
  • RG: Austin Barber
  • RT: Tytus Howard

After impressive starts to the season, we saw some struggles from the Browns’ rookie duo in left tackle Spencer Fano and right guard Austin Barber in the win over the Carolina Panthers. Fano allowed six pressures from 44 pass-blocking snaps on a 50.8 PFF pass-blocking grade, while Barber played to a 42.7 PFF pass-blocking grade due to three pressures.

30. Houston Texans (no change)

  • LT: Aireontae Ersery
  • LG: Keylan Rutledge
  • C: Evan Brown
  • RG: Ed Ingram/Wyatt Teller
  • RT: Trent Brown

The Texans had three offensive linemen register PFF pass-blocking grades above 80.0 in the loss to the Indianapolis Colts, with the trio of Aireontae Ersery, Trent Brown and Evan Brown combining to allow just two pressures in the game. The Colts had success going against both guards, with Wyatt Teller giving up two pressures and rookie Keylan Rutledge tagged with three. The question for this week is whether or not we see Ed Ingram back from injury.

31. Washington Commanders (no change)

  • LT: Brandon Coleman
  • LG: Chris Paul
  • C: Nick Allegretti
  • RG: Matt Gulbin
  • RT: Josh Conerly Jr.

Josh Conerly Jr. continues to be solid in pass protection, earning a 67.3 PFF pass-blocking grade in a game that saw him give up a pair of pressures from 38 pass-blocking snaps. Nick Allegretti had trouble at right guard, leading the team with six pressures and receiving a team-low 14.5 PFF pass-blocking grade.

32. Green Bay Packers (no change)

  • LT: Jordan Morgan
  • LG: Jager Burton
  • C: Sean Rhyan
  • RG: Jacob Monk
  • RT: Anthony Belton

With the number of injuries they have, it’s not yet clear exactly what the Packers’ offensive line will look like in Week 4. Green Bay just signed Mekhi Becton, but it’s unknown if the team views him as a guard or a tackle. The reality is that Becton would likely be an upgrade anywhere on the right side of this offensive line. The unit’s performance against the Atlanta Falcons on Thursday Night Football was a disaster, with the starting unit combining to allow 28 total pressures.
