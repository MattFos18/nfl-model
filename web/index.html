Where the data comes from

Plays, scores, schedules
nflverse play-by-play, schedules, injuries and depth charts (Model → Data pulls and verification lists every download with its hash).
Lines
ESPN's scoreboard feed and The Odds API (nine books), logged every 30 minutes by the line watch; the closing line for graded games is nflverse's.
Vegas on a card
The latest logged line (the consensus across sources at the last snapshot, every 30 minutes); the edge, the cover odds and the best number follow it. The Vegas win chance is the newest snapshot's moneylines with the vig removed (each book's pair, averaged), and the chip under it is the model's rounded win chance minus Vegas's. A flag and its bet stay as recorded at the weekly run, so a flagged card can show a line that has since moved.
Player prop lines
The Odds API's six core markets from every US book (Thursday evening and Sunday morning) and PrizePicks' full pick'em board every six hours (every market at even odds by construction, no touchdown markets, promo copies of a line dropped). A player's line is the median across the books' latest numbers; the opening line (the first one logged for the game) sits in the Book cell's hover. "No line" means no book has posted that market yet. Underdog refuses requests from the runner (426), so it is not a source yet.
Weather
Played games: the schedule's kickoff temperature and wind. Unplayed games: the Open-Meteo hourly forecast at the stadium, fetched on every weekly run (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, Sunday) and used only within 4 days of kickoff; further out the game is priced as typical weather (7 mph, not cold, dry).
Injuries
nflverse injury reports and weekly rosters (IR and the like): the starting QB listed out, the value of skill players out (player model), snap-weighted absences on offense and the opponent's defense.
Verification
2024 team totals against Pro-Football-Reference, scores against the schedule, offense and defense mirrors, and a leak test that corrupts future results and checks nothing changes.

What each number on a card means

Model points
Each team's expected points from the fitted equation: a base for a league-average team in a neutral game, plus each rating's distance from the league average times what it is worth, plus the situation (home, roof, wind, cold, rain, division) and the absences (QB out, skill value out, snaps out, offseason turnover early in the season). "Why these numbers" on any card lists every input; Model → How it was built prints the whole equation.
Model spread and total
The spread is home expected points minus away. The total comes from its own equation, fit to game totals from both teams' ratings, the QBs and the weather (it beat adding the two scores on both backtest windows), so the two team scores do not add exactly to it.
Vegas points
What the line implies: (total ± spread) ÷ 2.
Edge
Model line minus the Vegas line, in points, with the side it favours. The flag fires at : the cut that holds in both backtest windows (Results tab).
Win %
Model: the chance the final margin lands above zero when the margin is treated as a bell curve centered on the model spread with a 13-point spread (the actual miss over 2013 to 2025), reshaped so 3 and 7 get their true share. Vegas: the moneyline with the bookmaker's cut removed, so the two sides sum to 100%.
Cover %
What has actually happened: of every backtest game before this season where the model's edge was this size, the share where the model's side covered (a curve fitted across edge sizes, capped at 7 points). It replaces the model's own bell-curve figure, which runs about ten points hotter because the line knows things the model does not. Recomputed every run; the picks file carries both.
Edge at best number
When the books disagree, the model's side may get a better number at one book than the consensus line; the chip shows the edge at that number and where it is. The flag itself is decided on the consensus line, and the tracker records the best number as the line bet.
Stake
On a flagged spread: a quarter of the Kelly fraction from the calibrated cover odds at the best book's price (-110 when no price is logged), as a share of the bankroll. The calibrated odds are refit every run, so the same edge can carry a different stake week to week; a 4-point edge at 54% supports about 0.8%, at 53% about 0.4%. Full Kelly would be four times that and assumes the odds are exact. Recorded in the week's picks file.
Shadow rules
Rules logged and graded on the same games as the flag but never bet, shown only as a summary line under Rules compared on the live tab. Three today: a 4.5-point cut, the 4-point cut on underdogs only (the model's favorite-side flags have lost on two of the three backtest windows), and the 4-point cut in weeks 1 to 13 only (weeks 14 to 17 are the one stretch where the flag sits under break-even, and Week 18 is already skipped). Each rule's backtest record on the untouched, tuning and held-out windows sits in the same table. All three were found on the backtest, so none is bet until the live record says so; the decision is set for January 2027.
Over %
Calibrated the same way as the cover %, on the total edge.
Books now
Under "Why these numbers": every book's current spread and total from the last line snapshot, and the best available number for the model's side. A half point at a key number (3, 7) is worth real money over a season, so a flagged bet goes at the best price, not the first one. The full log (every book, every 30 minutes) is folded underneath; it grades closing line value on the Track record tab and, after a season, tests whether lines that move toward the model beat the ones that move away.
Matchup history
Last meetings, coaches, quarterbacks, stadium and form, built automatically on every weekly run from the nflverse schedule (every game since 2013 with its score, closing line, coaches and starting QBs). Nothing here changes the model's numbers: each of these was tested as an input and none improved the backtest (Decision log). It is context for reading a card, not an input.
Scheme matchup and How they play / defend
Readings from nflverse participation data (2016 on: the defense's man or zone call and coverage family, formation, personnel, box, rushers, pressure) and FTN charting (2022 on: motion, play action, RPO, screens, tempo, blitzers), joined to every play. Rates say how a team plays; EPA per play in each look says how it has gone, with the play count beside it and blank under 20 plays. This season is used once a team has 300 charted plays, otherwise last season, and the label says which. The current season's participation file arrives on nflverse during the season, so early-season profiles carry the FTN fields and leave coverage and pressure blank. None of it feeds the model's numbers; it is the first layer of the matchup work (docs section 17).
Season
Win totals, division, playoff and Super Bowl odds from playing the rest of the regular season out 10,000 times. Every remaining game gets an expected margin from the game model's own equation on each team's ratings as of this week (the coming week's games use the model's actual predictions, forecast and absences included; later games carry no forecast or absences, so wind sits at the league-typical 7 mph outdoors and no one is out). Margins are drawn with the model's residual scale; a margin within 0.07 of zero is a tie. Standings follow the league's order of tiebreakers as far as records go (head-to-head among the tied, division record, conference record, then a coin flip; the strength-of-victory rules beyond those are not applied). Seeds play the bracket with the higher seed at home and the Super Bowl on a neutral field. Backtest (Backtest → Season odds and totals): the same odds made as of weeks 1, 5, 9, 13 and 17 of 2019 to 2025 against what happened, beside naive baselines; the two knobs tested (shrinking future margins, widening the scale) helped the held-out window and hurt the tuning window, so neither is used. Player season totals: what he has so far plus his per-game mean against an average defense (the props rule's volume and rate, no game script, no median factor) times the games his team has left times a fitted share, blended with pace (his own per-game so far over the whole schedule); the share and the pace weight are fitted on 2016 to 2018 on the season-total error (receivers 0.65 and 0.5, rushers 0.625 and 0.5, passers 0.525 and 0.75), and the result beats pace and last season on both windows. The breakout flag is scored by how often it came true.
Every data store
Model → Every data store lists each file the model keeps, read from the files themselves on each build: raw downloads (what each holds, seasons, size, rows and columns, when last pulled), built tables (rows, columns, the step that writes them, where they show), the line, weather, tracker and run logs, the reports and the page's own files. Team → Scheme and play calling shows a team's offense and defense profile over its last 17 games against the league with its rank, and its EPA by the look it faced or played. No public source charts routes; the route-adjacent readings (coverage faced, motion, play action, depth of target, air yards) are there.
Tackle projections (props)
Tackles plus assists on defensive plays only, the rule some books settle on (bet365 among them). Others, FanDuel among them, also count special-teams tackles, which in 2025 added about a tenth of a tackle a game for a defender with four or more defensive tackles, and more for a core special-teams player. The game logs show the official total, special teams included.
EPA (expected points added)
Every down, distance, yard line, time left and score has an expected number of points for the team with the ball: what teams in that spot went on to score, on average, before the other side next scored (nflverse's model, fitted on every play since 1999). A play's EPA is expected points after it minus before it. Near midfield (2016 to 2025 plays), a 6-yard gain on 3rd and 5 moves the chains and adds about +1.4. The same 6 yards on 3rd and 8 falls short and costs about -0.7. An interception averages about -4.4. A player's EPA is the sum over his plays: a receiver's targets (catch or not), a back's carries, a quarterback's dropbacks (passes, sacks and scrambles). Per play is that sum over the plays. Team ratings, the QB rating and every player value are all built from it.
Player game logs (Players → a player)
Every regular-season and playoff game since 2016, one row per game. The box score follows the official one: passing yards are the yards on completions (a sack's lost yards are not passing yards), a kneel-down is a carry, a spike is a pass attempt, and two-point tries are not plays. Every run checks the logs against nflverse's official player stats, and they tie. Tackles, sacks, interceptions and passes defended are the official numbers, special-teams tackles included. Targets, air yards, yards after the catch, red-zone looks, first downs and EPA come from nflverse play-by-play; snaps from nflverse snap counts; drops, broken tackles, yards before and after contact, bad throws, pressures, blitzes, hits and the coverage he allowed from Pro-Football-Reference's charting (2018 on). Against man, zone, pressure and light or heavy boxes come from nflverse's participation charting, which is published some time after a season ends. Until then those columns show a chip saying which seasons are charted, and they fill in on the run after it appears. Career by season sums the same logs.
Against an average defense (player pages)
EPA is the play-by-play's expected points added (nflfastR): it scores each play by the down, distance and field position before and after, and does not know who the defense was. This tile re-scores each of his past games against an average defense: the game's EPA per play less what that defense allowed per pass play (per run for a rusher) coming in, against the league. It predicts a player's next game a little better than the raw number for passers, receivers and rushers on every test window, so it is shown; the game model keeps the raw value, where the adjustment made no difference.
Vs an average starter (Rankings → Positions, Players, Team overview)
How many expected points a player adds per play against an average starter at his position, the same bar for every team: the median of the league's starters (each team's QB with the most dropbacks over its last 17 games; its top five skill players by share of the team's touches; top five linemen and top eleven defenders by snap share; its kicker and punter). 0 is an average starter, plus is better, minus worse. Quarterbacks: the model's QB rating (expected points per play on passes, sacks, scrambles and designed runs, weighted toward recent games and pulled toward a backup's level until he has enough plays) minus the median starter's. Points a game is that times the model's fitted points per unit of QB rating (about 15 this season), so it is what the model would move a line against an average starter; it does not use plays a game, which mostly reflect pace and game script. "Points if out" for skill players is different on purpose: it is what the team loses to the player who takes his touches, a replacement-level player.
Card tabs
Each game card opens one panel at a time: Takeaways (the handful of readings on the card that bear on the outcome: the model against the market, the largest inputs and absences, the scheme look that matters most each way, the last meetings, the biggest prop leans against the book), Why these numbers (every input and its points), Matchup history, Scheme matchup and Player props. Only the spread flag is a bet; the rest are readings.
Rankings, Positions
Every rostered player valued as of the coming week (his last eight games, across seasons) ranked within his position group (value against an average starter at it, EPA per play terms; Team → Players for each group's basis), with coverage numbers for corners and safeties. Defenders are grouped as edge rushers, interior line, linebackers, corners and safeties (depth chart; a linebacker who pressures on 1.5%+ of his snaps is an edge) and valued by what their plays were worth: coverage yards saved, interceptions, pressures, run stops (weights measured on 2016-18 plays; it predicts a defense better than credited tackles on both windows). Corners are valued on coverage per target (yards saved and interceptions per ball thrown their way, at a typical corner's target volume, with a long memory), which predicts a corner's next season best and lines up with the published consensus lists far better than per-snap numbers did. Linemen are rated with their unit (pressures allowed, yards before contact), since no public data splits a line's blocking by player.
Players against this defense (projections)
Under Player props on each card: every rostered QB, receiver and rusher with a profile (last 17 games, any team), his split against the looks this defense plays (man and zone, pressure, light and heavy boxes, 15 plays minimum, shown as readings), and a projection by the rule six rounds of walk-forward backtest chose on both windows (2019 to 2025, every constant fitted on 2016 to 2018; docs section 18). Volume: the team's plays per game over its last 17, moved by the game script (a fitted line on the closing spread and total: favorites run more and pass less, high totals add pass plays), shared among the players who are playing in proportion to usage, where usage is decayed 0.85 per game back so the current role counts most (a listed-out player's share goes to his teammates, and he is shown at what he would have projected). Rate: his yards per touch shrunk toward the league and moved a little toward what the defense allows. Line: volume x rate x a median factor (receivers , rushers , QBs ), because yards in a game are right-skewed and the line that is off by least sits below the mean, as a book's over/under does; the mean is in the tooltip. Passing yards also blend the team's dropbacks a quarter of the way toward what the opponent allows per game and drop 0.5% per mph of kickoff wind above 10 once a forecast is usable (round four: both helped passing on both windows; for receiving and rushing every round-four layer, including the QB's rating, home field, pace and wind, was inside the noise). Each team's players are then moved toward what the game model's expected points say the team should produce (its expected receiving, rushing and passing yards and touchdowns, fitted on 2016 to 2018): yards a quarter of the way, passing half; touchdowns half, passing all the way (round six: helps every stat on both windows, passing yards by three; the game model's margin in place of the closing line changed nothing). Receptions: targets x his catch rate shrunk toward the league ( targets of weight) x . Touchdowns: volume x his rate shrunk toward the league (200 targets, 200 carries, 400 dropbacks of weight), receiving and passing scores moved 2% per point of expected margin; interceptions at the league rate, his own rate carrying no information (round five, by absolute error for receptions and Poisson fit for scores, both windows). Backtest error on receptions: catches per player-game. Projecting from the look-by-look splits, routes, coverage-specific usage, defense by position or the coach's pass rate was worse than not, so they stay readings. Backtest error per player-game: receiving yards, rushing , passing (2019-22 / 2023-25). Break-even
52.4% at −110. A cover or over % below that is a losing bet even if the model is right on average.
