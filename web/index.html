Vegas on a card
The latest logged line (the median across sources at the last snapshot, to the half point); the edge, the cover odds and the best number follow it. The Vegas win chance is the newest snapshot's moneylines with the vig removed (each book's pair, averaged), and the chip under it is the model's rounded win chance minus Vegas's. The model's win chance is calibrated (a logistic mapping of the raw chance, fit on the seasons before this one and refit every run; the raw chance ran hot when the home side was a slight underdog), the raw figure in the bar's tooltip; the season odds still simulate on the raw chance. A flag and its bet stay as recorded at the weekly run, so a flagged card can show a line that has since moved.

What each number on a card means

Model points
Each team's expected points from the fitted equation: a base for a league-average team in a neutral game, plus each rating's distance from the league average times what it is worth, plus the situation (home, roof, wind, cold, rain, division) and the absences (QB out, skill value out, snaps out, offseason turnover early in the season). "Why these numbers" on any card lists every input; Model → How it was built prints the whole equation.
Model spread and total
The spread is home expected points minus away. The total comes from its own equation, fit to game totals from both teams' ratings, the QBs and the weather (it beat adding the two scores on both backtest windows), so the two team scores do not add exactly to it.
Vegas points
What the line implies: (total ± spread) ÷ 2.
Edge
Model line minus the Vegas line, in points, with the side it favours. The flag fires at : the cut that holds in both backtest windows (Results tab).

Edge at best number
When the books disagree, the model's side may get a better number at one book than the consensus line; the chip shows the edge at that number and where it is. The flag itself is decided on the consensus line, and the tracker records the best number as the line bet.

Books now
Under "Score projection": every book's current spread and total from the last line snapshot, and the best available number for the model's side. A half point at a key number (3, 7) is worth real money over a season, so a flagged bet goes at the best price, not the first one. The full log (every book, every snapshot) is folded underneath; it grades closing line value on the Track record tab and, after a season, tests whether lines that move toward the model beat the ones that move away.
Matchup history
Last meetings, coaches, quarterbacks, stadium and form, built automatically on every weekly run from the nflverse schedule (every game since with its score, closing line, coaches and starting QBs). Nothing here changes the model's numbers: each of these was tested as an input and none improved the backtest (Decision log). It is context for reading a card, not an input.
Scheme matchup and How they play / defend
Readings from nflverse participation data ( on: the defense's man or zone call and coverage family, formation, personnel, box, rushers, pressure) and FTN charting ( on: motion, play action, RPO, screens, tempo, blitzers), joined to every play. Rates say how a team plays; EPA per play in each look says how it has gone, with the play count beside it and blank under plays. This season is used once a team has charted plays, otherwise last season, and the label says which. The current season's participation file arrives on nflverse during the season, so early-season profiles carry the FTN fields and leave coverage and pressure blank. None of it feeds the model's numbers; it is the first layer of the matchup work (docs section 17).

Every data store
Model → Every data store lists each file the model keeps, read from the files themselves on each build: raw downloads (what each holds, seasons, size, rows and columns, when last pulled), built tables (rows, columns, the step that writes them, where they show), the line, weather, tracker and run logs, the reports and the page's own files. No public source charts routes; the route-adjacent readings (coverage faced, motion, play action, depth of target, air yards) are there.
Tackle projections (props)
Tackles plus assists on defensive plays only, the rule some books settle on (bet365 among them). Others, FanDuel among them, also count special-teams tackles, which adds a little for most defenders and more for a core special-teams player. The game logs show the official total, special teams included.
EPA (expected points added)
Every down, distance, yard line, time left and score has an expected number of points for the team with the ball: what teams in that spot went on to score, on average, before the other side next scored (nflverse's model, fitted on every play since 1999). A play's EPA is expected points after it minus before it: a gain that moves the chains adds, the same gain short of the sticks costs, and an interception costs most. A player's EPA is the sum over his plays: a receiver's targets (catch or not), a back's carries, a quarterback's dropbacks (passes, sacks and scrambles). Per play is that sum over the plays. Team ratings, the QB rating and every player value are all built from it.
Player game logs (Players → a player)
Every regular-season and playoff game since , one row per game. The box score follows the official one: passing yards are the yards on completions (a sack's lost yards are not passing yards), a kneel-down is a carry, a spike is a pass attempt, and two-point tries are not plays. Every run checks the logs against nflverse's official player stats, and they tie. Tackles, sacks, interceptions and passes defended are the official numbers, special-teams tackles included. Targets, air yards, yards after the catch, red-zone looks, first downs and EPA come from nflverse play-by-play; snaps from nflverse snap counts; drops, broken tackles, yards before and after contact, bad throws, pressures, blitzes, hits and the coverage he allowed from Pro-Football-Reference's charting ( on). Against man, zone, pressure and light or heavy boxes come from nflverse's participation charting, which is published some time after a season ends. Until then those columns show a chip saying which seasons are charted, and they fill in on the run after it appears. Career by season sums the same logs.
Against an average defense (player pages)
EPA is the play-by-play's expected points added (nflfastR): it scores each play by the down, distance and field position before and after, and does not know who the defense was. This tile re-scores each of his past games against an average defense: the game's EPA per play less what that defense allowed per pass play (per run for a rusher) coming in, against the league. It predicts a player's next game a little better than the raw number for passers, receivers and rushers on every test window, so it is shown; the game model keeps the raw value, where the adjustment made no difference.
Vs an average starter (Rankings → Positions, Players, Team overview)
How many expected points a player adds per play against an average starter at his position, the same bar for every team: the median of the league's starters (). 0 is an average starter, plus is better, minus worse. Quarterbacks: the model's QB rating (expected points per play on passes, sacks, scrambles and designed runs, weighted toward recent games and pulled toward a backup's level until he has enough plays) minus the median starter's. Points a game is that times the model's fitted points per unit of QB rating ( in the fit that priced this week), so it is what the model would move a line against an average starter; it does not use plays a game, which mostly reflect pace and game script. "Points if out" for skill players is different on purpose: it is what the team loses to the player who takes his touches, a replacement-level player.
Card tabs
Each game card opens one panel at a time: Score projection (every input and its points), Injury report, Matchup history (head to head and each team's form over the last 10), Scheme matchup (each defense's looks, how often it plays them, the offense's EPA gap to the league in each, and the use-weighted sum that sets the verdict) and Player props. Only the spread flag and the totals flag are bets; the rest are readings.
Rankings, Positions
Every rostered player valued as of the coming week (his last games, across seasons) ranked within his position group (value against an average starter at it, EPA per play terms; Team → Players for each group's basis), with coverage numbers for corners and safeties. Defenders are grouped as edge rushers, interior line, linebackers, corners and safeties (depth chart; a linebacker who pressures on + of his snaps is an edge) and valued by what their plays were worth: coverage yards saved, interceptions, pressures, run stops (it predicts a defense better than credited tackles on both windows). Corners are valued on coverage per target (yards saved and interceptions per ball thrown their way, at a typical corner's target volume, with a long memory), which predicts a corner's next season best and lines up with the published consensus lists far better than per-snap numbers did. Linemen are rated with their unit (pressures allowed, yards before contact), since no public data splits a line's blocking by player.
